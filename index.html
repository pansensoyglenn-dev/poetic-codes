The Commonplace

A running record of what's worth thinking about — history, philosophy, economy, code, culture, and the world as it turns.

An open commonplace book — illustrated banner

For centuries, readers have kept commonplace books — private notebooks where a passage worth remembering, a half-formed argument, or a stray observation was copied down and filed by theme, so that a lifetime of reading and thinking could be found again later. This is that practice, made public.

What you'll find here moves across history, philosophy, economy, programming, culture, food, local and global politics, weather, farming, artificial intelligence, and music videos — not because these fields have much in common on the surface, but because a mind paying attention doesn't stay in one lane. There's no editorial calendar and no algorithm deciding what comes next — just a running record of what seemed worth thinking about, written down before it was forgotten.
